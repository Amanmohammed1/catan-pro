# M6 — Seafarers

**Goal (PLAN.md):** the first expansion, and the first real test of whether the
`RuleModule` interface is right. Ships, gold fields, the pirate, island victory
points, a route that counts both roads and ships, and the scenario boards to
play them on.

## What was built

### The interface, widened first (ADR 0007)

`RuleModule` had three members and ADR 0005 had already predicted that would be
too narrow. It now has nine, and each one is there because a named expansion
needs it — `setupState` for module-owned state, `onDiceRoll` and `onPhaseEnter`
for the Cities & Knights turn, `interceptAction`, `scoreContribution` and
`reducers` for both.

The expansion actions stay in the engine's central `Action`, `GameEvent` and
`Phase` unions rather than the unions being made open. That is deliberate:
`packages/protocol` carries a compile-time check that the wire schema matches the
engine's action union exactly, and `legalMoves`/`reduce` are exhaustive switches.
An open union would discard all three safety nets. Golden rule 7 is satisfied by
dispatch going through the registry, which it does.

### The mechanics

- **Ships** on sea and coast edges, with `buildShip` and `moveShip`. A ship
  records the turn it was built, because p.2 forbids moving one the same turn.
- **Gold fields** grant a choice, so they cannot resolve silently — production
  enters a `gainGold` phase enumerated by `legalMoves`, exactly as Year of Plenty
  already was.
- **The pirate**, as board state that ship placement already respects.
- **Island victory points**, derived from the board rather than tracked, because
  the award is once per island per player and nothing ever removes a settlement
  (ADR 0008).
- **Longest Route**, walking roads and ships together — but joined only at your
  own buildings, per p.2.

### The boards

Generated by `scripts/generate-seafarers-scenarios.mjs` from the rulebook's own
**Variable Setup** tables, not transcribed from its printed diagrams. The
diagrams exist only as images, and transcribing eight of them by eye is the
error source `generate-classic-scenario.mjs` was written to avoid (ADR 0008).

| Board                  | Seats | Land hexes | Discs | VP  |
| ---------------------- | ----- | ---------- | ----- | --- |
| `new-shores-3`         | 3     | 22         | 22    | 14  |
| `new-shores-4`         | 3–4   | 28         | 27    | 14  |
| `through-the-desert-3` | 3     | 25         | 22    | 14  |
| `through-the-desert-4` | 3–4   | 30         | 27    | 14  |
| `fog-islands-3`        | 3     | 14 + 12    | 14    | 12  |
| `fog-islands-4`        | 3–4   | 17 + 12    | 17    | 12  |

The Fog Islands rows read "face-up + face-down": twelve of its hexes start as
empty spaces and are dealt from a stack during play, so the disc column counts
only the discs laid at setup. Ten more wait in a second face-down pile, one for
each land hex still hidden.

`seafarers-board.test.ts` checks each board against the rulebook's composition
table — terrain multiset, disc count, which hexes stay bare, that gold is
numbered, the port mix, the island payouts and the opening restriction — across
many seeds. Parsing was already covered; this is what proves they are the _right_
boards rather than merely valid ones.

The sea counts deliberately do not match the printed tables: the rulebook's sea
count belongs to its own fixed island outlines, and ours are generated, so the
frame of water is whatever the shape needs. Every land figure is the rulebook's
exactly.

### The Fog Islands, and a board that changes (ADR 0009)

The last scenario is the only one that needed new machinery rather than new
data. It deals twelve of its hexes face down and fills a space when a player
builds beside it (p.8), which contradicts the rule that the board graph is built
once and never touched again.

What that cost:

- **`revealHex()`**, the single sanctioned exception. It rewrites the tile's
  slot, terrain and number, and reclassifies the six edges it touches — no id
  changes, so ADR 0001's guarantee that event-log ids stay valid survives.
- **`fog` as a terrain and a slot.** Widening the union made the compiler name
  all eight exhaustive sites that had to decide what an unknown hex looks like.
- **`afterAction`**, a new `RuleModule` hook. Revealing fires on a road _or_ a
  ship, and those are a central reducer case and a module-owned one
  respectively, so nothing existing could see both.
- **Redaction that matters.** The face-down piles live beside `devDeck` and
  `playerView` sends counts alone. The server's leak test now plays a Fog
  Islands board, because asserting this on the classic board would have passed
  over an empty set.

A hidden cost surfaced too: `setup.placeOn` had been declared, validated and
ignored since M0. Every earlier board either is all land or confines the opening
to a named island, so nothing noticed — but Fog Islands sets no island
restriction (p.8), and the fuzzer stalled all twenty smoke games at once. A
settlement went up on an intersection ringed by sea, where no setup road is
legal. The field is read now.

### Everywhere else

The client draws all of it — sea renders as water rather than as blue land,
ships float on it, the pirate sails, gold fields have controls. The lobby gained
a board picker that filters by seat count, so the new maps are selectable online;
`?hotseat=1&scenario=through-the-desert-4` reaches one without a server.

## What the fuzzer found

Registering a Seafarers board let the fuzzer reach this code for the first time,
and it immediately found three defects — all of them things the unit tests had
been written to confirm rather than to challenge. Roads could be built onto edges
already carrying a ship; a ship did not connect a settlement, which made 14% of
four-player games mathematically unwinnable rather than merely wrong; and the
harness could not tell a stalled game from a slow one. ADR 0008 records all
three, and the method note that matters: raising the action cap would have turned
the gate green and hidden the second one.

## What playing it found

The fuzzer proves a game runs to a winner without breaking an invariant. It
cannot say the rules are the _right_ rules, and it never looks at a screen. Two
bugs survived every gate below and turned up in the first few minutes of somebody
actually playing:

**Road Building only offered roads.** Seafarers p.3 widens the card — "2 roads,
2 ships, or 1 road and 1 ship at no cost". The card is base-game machinery and
the ship belongs to a module, and nothing tested the seam between them: it was
offered only when a road could be placed, the phase enumerated only roads, and
the module's own guard refused any build outside the main phase, so a ship would
have been rejected even if offered. Four places, one rule.

**A revealed hex never reached the player.** The server turned it face up
correctly every time. `WireView` omitted the board and `broadcastUpdate` stripped
it, on the stated grounds that "the board is static for a match" — true until
this milestone added a scenario that deals part of its board during play.
Twenty-one engine tests passed throughout, because they assert on engine state
and never cross the wire. Both the real client and the test client cached a board
they were never told had changed.

Two more turned up in the next session of play, and they share a cause: **a
control that hides itself instead of explaining itself.**

**The Ship control vanished when no ship was placeable.** It rendered only
inside `can("buildShip") && …`, which made its own `disabled` prop permanently
false and put its reason string — "No sea path connects to one of your ships or
buildings" — permanently out of reach. Somebody wrote the explanation and then
made it unreachable. On Through the Desert with every settlement inland, the
row simply disappeared, so there was no way to tell whether the board had ships
or the game had forgotten them. Road, Settlement and City all render always and
grey out; Ship now does too.

**The whole Board field vanished at five and six seats.** It was wrapped in
`boards.length > 1`, and only `classic-5-6` seats that many, so choosing five or
six removed the field entirely — reading as "no boards" rather than "one board".

Both had tests nearby that could never have caught them. The ship test asserted
`sawShipControl || armedAndPlaced`, satisfied as soon as the control appeared at
all — which under the old code meant _after_ a ship became legal. The board
picker test only asserted which boards were **absent** at six seats, which holds
just as well when the field is not rendered. Both are tightened, and a third
test now pins the real invariant: wherever Road is offered, Ship is offered too.

A rule-by-rule pass over p.2 and p.3 followed. Found correct and left alone:

| Rule                                                                 | Where                           |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| Gold pays a settlement 1 card of choice, a city 2 (p.2)              | `production.ts`                 |
| The pirate does not block production; only the robber does (p.2)     | `production.ts`                 |
| A ship connects to your ship or building, never a road (p.2)         | `canPlaceShip`                  |
| Ships and roads may not share a coastal edge — both ways (p.2)       | `canPlaceRoad` + `canPlaceShip` |
| No ship on an edge of the pirate's hex (p.2)                         | `canPlaceShip`                  |
| No building a ship past an opponent's building (p.2)                 | `canPlaceShip`                  |
| All four move-ship restrictions (p.2)                                | `canMoveShip`                   |
| A Knight offers the robber **or** the pirate (p.3)                   | `extraLegalMoves`               |
| The pirate steals from a player with a **ship** on that hex (p.2)    | `enterSteal`                    |
| Longest Route joins roads and ships only at your own buildings (p.2) | `longestRoad.ts`                |

Still open, both recorded rather than quietly skipped: the pirate cannot be sent
"to the frame" (p.2) — our board has no frame, and `movePirate` requires a sea
hex — and revealing a gold field pays no card (ADR 0009).

## Acceptance

| Check                                                   | Result                                      |
| ------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| `pnpm verify`                                           | green — 679 fast tests, 17 slow             |
| `pnpm fuzz` (classic, 4 players)                        | 10,000 games, 10,190,192 actions, 0 stalled |
| `pnpm fuzz --players 4 --scenario through-the-desert-4` | 10,000 games, 18.83M actions, 0 stalled     |
| `pnpm fuzz --players 3 --scenario through-the-desert-3` | 10,000 games, 19.21M actions, 0 stalled     |
| `pnpm fuzz --players 4 --scenario new-shores-4`         | 10,000 games, 17.14M actions, 0 stalled     |
| `pnpm fuzz --players 3 --scenario new-shores-3`         | 10,000 games, 17.55M actions, 0 stalled     |
| `pnpm fuzz --players 4 --scenario fog-islands-4`        | 10,000 games, 14.59M actions, 0 stalled     |
| `pnpm fuzz --players 3 --scenario fog-islands-3`        | 10,000 games, 14.17M actions, 0 stalled     |

The classic figure is the one that matters most for confidence that nothing
regressed: **10,190,192 actions, byte-identical** to the run recorded before
Through the Desert existed. Adding a board changed nothing for a game that does
not load the module.

Three games out of 60,000 hit the action cap with no winner — two on Through the
Desert at four players, one on New Shores at four. `exhausted`, not `stalled`,
at 0.005% against a 10% allowance, worst case 5,015 turns. ADR 0008 explains why
that reads as long games rather than the unwinnable-game defect it superficially
resembles.

Neither Fog Islands board produced a single one, which fits: it wins at twelve
points rather than fourteen, so games close out before they can run long.

Both Heading for New Shores boards were re-run here from their original 300- and
500-game figures, so every Seafarers board now meets golden rule 8's 10,000
rather than two of them being grandfathered in at the smaller numbers.

Wins spread evenly across seats on all four boards (2543/2501/2435/2519 and
3400/3271/3329 on Through the Desert, 2476/2529/2442/2552 and 3239/3309/3452 on
New Shores), which is the signal that neither the island victory points nor the
main-island opening restriction favours a position.

## Notes for later

- **Revealing a gold field pays no resource card.** p.8 says the finder takes "1
  resource card of the type produced by that hex", and gold produces a card of
  the player's choice (p.2) — which needs the `gainGold` phase. A _setup_ road
  can trigger a discovery, and taking over the phase machine mid-setup is not
  safe untested. Two of the twelve face-down hexes are gold, so this is not
  negligible; ADR 0009 records it.
- **Four Islands is declined, not pending.** p.6 gives each player their own home
  islands; a scenario pins one `vpForFirstSettlement` per island for everyone. It
  would load, build and fuzz clean while scoring wrongly. ADR 0008 says what it
  would take — recording the turn a building was placed, so setup-turn buildings
  identify each player's home islands.
- **`startingPieces` is declared, validated and ignored.** Seafarers p.3 wants a
  ship placed instead of a road beside a coastal starting settlement, which is
  exactly what the field is for.
- The Seafarers + Cities & Knights combination rules are not in scope.
