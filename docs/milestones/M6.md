# M6 — Seafarers

**Goal (PLAN.md):** the first expansion, and the first real test of whether the
`RuleModule` interface is right. Ships, gold fields, the pirate, island victory
points, a route that counts both roads and ships, and the scenario boards to
play them on.

## What was built

### The interface, widened first (ADR 0007)

`RuleModule` had three members and ADR 0005 had already predicted that would be
too narrow. It now has nine, and each one is there because a named expansion
needs it — `setupState` for module-owned state, `onDiceRoll` and `onPhaseEnter`
for the Cities & Knights turn, `interceptAction`, `scoreContribution` and
`reducers` for both.

The expansion actions stay in the engine's central `Action`, `GameEvent` and
`Phase` unions rather than the unions being made open. That is deliberate:
`packages/protocol` carries a compile-time check that the wire schema matches the
engine's action union exactly, and `legalMoves`/`reduce` are exhaustive switches.
An open union would discard all three safety nets. Golden rule 7 is satisfied by
dispatch going through the registry, which it does.

### The mechanics

- **Ships** on sea and coast edges, with `buildShip` and `moveShip`. A ship
  records the turn it was built, because p.2 forbids moving one the same turn.
- **Gold fields** grant a choice, so they cannot resolve silently — production
  enters a `gainGold` phase enumerated by `legalMoves`, exactly as Year of Plenty
  already was.
- **The pirate**, as board state that ship placement already respects.
- **Island victory points**, derived from the board rather than tracked, because
  the award is once per island per player and nothing ever removes a settlement
  (ADR 0008).
- **Longest Route**, walking roads and ships together — but joined only at your
  own buildings, per p.2.

### The boards

Generated by `scripts/generate-seafarers-scenarios.mjs` from the rulebook's own
**Variable Setup** tables, not transcribed from its printed diagrams. The
diagrams exist only as images, and transcribing eight of them by eye is the
error source `generate-classic-scenario.mjs` was written to avoid (ADR 0008).

| Board                  | Seats | Land hexes | Discs | VP  |
| ---------------------- | ----- | ---------- | ----- | --- |
| `new-shores-3`         | 3     | 22         | 22    | 14  |
| `new-shores-4`         | 3–4   | 28         | 27    | 14  |
| `through-the-desert-3` | 3     | 25         | 22    | 14  |
| `through-the-desert-4` | 3–4   | 30         | 27    | 14  |

`seafarers-board.test.ts` checks each board against the rulebook's composition
table — terrain multiset, disc count, which hexes stay bare, that gold is
numbered, the port mix, the island payouts and the opening restriction — across
many seeds. Parsing was already covered; this is what proves they are the _right_
boards rather than merely valid ones.

The sea counts deliberately do not match the printed tables: the rulebook's sea
count belongs to its own fixed island outlines, and ours are generated, so the
frame of water is whatever the shape needs. Every land figure is the rulebook's
exactly.

### Everywhere else

The client draws all of it — sea renders as water rather than as blue land,
ships float on it, the pirate sails, gold fields have controls. The lobby gained
a board picker that filters by seat count, so the new maps are selectable online;
`?hotseat=1&scenario=through-the-desert-4` reaches one without a server.

## What the fuzzer found

Registering a Seafarers board let the fuzzer reach this code for the first time,
and it immediately found three defects — all of them things the unit tests had
been written to confirm rather than to challenge. Roads could be built onto edges
already carrying a ship; a ship did not connect a settlement, which made 14% of
four-player games mathematically unwinnable rather than merely wrong; and the
harness could not tell a stalled game from a slow one. ADR 0008 records all
three, and the method note that matters: raising the action cap would have turned
the gate green and hidden the second one.

## Acceptance

| Check                                                   | Result                                      |
| ------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| `pnpm verify`                                           | green — 655 fast tests, 17 slow             |
| `pnpm fuzz` (classic, 4 players)                        | 10,000 games, 10,190,192 actions, 0 stalled |
| `pnpm fuzz --players 4 --scenario through-the-desert-4` | 10,000 games, 18.83M actions, 0 stalled     |
| `pnpm fuzz --players 3 --scenario through-the-desert-3` | 10,000 games, 19.21M actions, 0 stalled     |

The classic figure is the one that matters most for confidence that nothing
regressed: **10,190,192 actions, byte-identical** to the run recorded before
Through the Desert existed. Adding a board changed nothing for a game that does
not load the module.

Through the Desert at four players had 2 games of 10,000 hit the action cap with
no winner — `exhausted`, not `stalled`, at 0.02% against a 10% allowance, with a
worst case of 4,858 turns. ADR 0008 explains why that reads as long games rather
than the unwinnable-game defect it superficially resembles.

Wins spread evenly across seats on both boards (2543/2501/2435/2519 and
3400/3271/3329), which is the signal that neither the island victory points nor
the main-island opening restriction favours a position.

## Notes for later

- **Fog Islands** is the remaining scenario with teeth. It needs `hiddenStacks`
  to become genuine hidden information, which brings `playerView` redaction work
  and an extension to the server's leak test with it.
- **Four Islands is declined, not pending.** p.6 gives each player their own home
  islands; a scenario pins one `vpForFirstSettlement` per island for everyone. It
  would load, build and fuzz clean while scoring wrongly. ADR 0008 says what it
  would take — recording the turn a building was placed, so setup-turn buildings
  identify each player's home islands.
- **`startingPieces` is declared, validated and ignored.** Seafarers p.3 wants a
  ship placed instead of a road beside a coastal starting settlement, which is
  exactly what the field is for.
- The Seafarers + Cities & Knights combination rules are not in scope.
